Chapter 11

The Controls page.

Controls is a grid of camera settings with a record card at the top. Every tile reads a setting from the camera and, where the camera allows, writes it back. This chapter lists each tile, its options, when it is greyed out, and what it is for.

How the page works

  • Two groups. Shoot holds the Exposure, Capture and Focus sections. Audio / TC holds the Audio and Timecode sections and appears only in video mode on Nikon cameras. RED cameras show their own Exposure and Capture tiles and no Focus or Audio / TC group.
  • Photo or video follows the camera. The app reads the camera's photo/video selector and swaps the tiles to match. There is no switch in the app. A PHOTO or VIDEO badge in the status strip shows which the camera is in.
  • Pickers list only what the camera allows. Tap a tile to open a list with the current value ticked. A setting with only two values toggles directly without a list.
  • Grey means the camera owns it. A grey tile is one the camera reports as read only in its current mode. Tapping it explains why with one of these notices:
    • Not reported by this camera: the body does not expose the setting.
    • Controlled by the camera: the setting is fixed by a physical dial or the exposure mode. Check the camera.
    • Read-only in the current mode: the camera allows several values but not right now, for example during recording.
    • Set on camera dial: the exposure mode is chosen by the mode dial (Z6III, Zf, Z7II).
    • Locked while recording: ISO in R3D while rolling.
  • Refused writes are explained. If the camera rejects a change, a yellow notice at the bottom of the grid says "The camera is busy", "The camera did not accept that value in its current mode", "The camera has locked this setting in its current mode" or "The camera did not apply the change". On the Zf and Zfc a refused exposure write instead offers to turn on Remote Mode (see below).
  • Landscape shows the record card and grid on the left and a metadata panel on the right with the camera name, transport, battery, Codec and Res (or Quality and Area in photo mode), a LENS row with focal length and maximum aperture, and the card storage bars.

Exposure section

Mode

Options
M, P, A, S
Shows
Also Auto and U1 to U4 with the underlying mode, for example U2 · M

The exposure program. The picker offers only the four PASM modes; user modes and Auto are shown but set on the camera. Bodies whose mode dial owns the setting show "Set on camera dial". In video with the R3D codec the mode is fixed at M.

When to use it

Shoot video in M so exposure does not drift during a take. Changing the mode re-reads aperture, shutter, ISO, white balance and metering so the tiles match the new mode.

Aperture

Options
The lens's steps as the camera lists them, for example F2.8, F4

Separate photo and video properties, so the value can differ between modes. Writable only in A and M; the camera greys it otherwise. Manual-aperture lenses and lenses without electronics show nothing to set.

Base ISO Video ZR

Options
Low, High (toggle)

Switches the sensor between its two native ISOs. Effective only with the R3D codec on the Nikon ZR; other codecs ignore the tap. The exposure tools recalibrate to the new base.

When to use it

Low (800) for daylight and lit interiors, High (6400) for genuinely dark scenes. Change the base, then set the ISO; values near a base are cleanest. See Cinematography basics.

Angle / Speed

Options
The camera's list. Speed as 1/50, 2s, Bulb or Time; angle as 180°

One tile that shows the shutter as a speed, or as an angle when the camera is in angle mode. Writable in S and M. If the camera reports no list, a fallback of common speeds from 1/25 to 1/8000 is offered.

Shtr Mode Video

Options
Speed, Angle (toggle)
Bodies
ZR, Z8, Z9, Z6III and other bodies that report shutter angle

Switches the camera between shutter speed and shutter angle. Choosing Angle keeps motion blur constant when you change frame rate; see the 180-degree rule.

ISO

Options
The values the camera allows in the current codec and format, between 25 and 409600

The list is re-read every time you open it, because the allowed values change with codec (N-Log, HLG and RAW narrow the list) and with the base ISO. In video with Auto ISO on the camera, the tile reads Auto 1600 with the effective value; the picker still lists manual values. In R3D the ISO is locked while recording. On the Zf and Zfc the camera refuses ISO changes unless Remote Mode is on.

Exp±

Options
The camera's range in thirds of a stop: -2/3, ±0, +1 1/3

Exposure compensation for the automatic modes. Greyed in M because there is nothing to compensate.

Meter

Photo
Matrix, Center, Spot, Highlight
Video
Matrix, Center, HiLight

Which part of the frame the camera's meter weighs. Highlight-weighted protects the brightest areas in automatic modes.

Remote Mode Zf, Zfc New in 2.1

Options
On ("App sets exposure"), Off ("Camera dials")
Default
Off

The Zf and Zfc have physical dials for shutter speed, ISO and exposure compensation, and in their normal remote mode the camera refuses exposure changes from an app. Turning Remote Mode on tells the camera to take exposure from the app and ignore its dials. The first time the camera refuses a write the app asks "Turn On Remote Mode?"; the same switch is under Connection › Advanced as Zf Remote-Only Controls. It cannot be changed while recording.

When to use it

Turn it on when you want to set exposure from the phone. Leave it off when the operator uses the dials and the phone is only a monitor.

Capture section

Video order: WB, Kelvin, Tint, IS / VR, Res/FPS, Codec. Photo order: WB, Kelvin, Quality, RAW, Size, Area, Picture, Release.

WB

Options
Auto, Natural Auto, Sunny, Cloudy, Shade, Incan, Fluor, Flash, K, PRE, Same Photo, as the camera lists them

In video, Auto expands into the camera's Auto 0, Auto 1 and Auto 2 variants (keep white, keep warm, keep warmer). In R3D the automatic modes are removed because the camera does not allow them. Choose K to unlock the Kelvin and Tint tiles.

When to use it

Set K and a fixed Kelvin for every video scene. Use PRE (a measured preset from a grey card set on the camera) for mixed light. See White balance.

Kelvin

Options
The camera's range in 100 K steps

Active only when WB is K; otherwise the tile reads --. Separate photo and video values.

Tint Video

Range
Amber–blue A6 to B6 in 0.5 steps; green–magenta G6 to M6 in 0.25 steps

Opens the full-screen WB Fine Tune grid: tap or drag in the colour field, or nudge one step with the G, A, B and M pad buttons. The tile shows the offset, for example A2 M1.5. Available when the camera reports the movie tint as writable.

When to use it

Fluorescent and cheap LED light leaves a green or magenta cast that Kelvin cannot fix. Put a grey card in the shot, open the RGB parade, and move along the G–M axis until the three traces line up.

IS / VR Video

Options
Off, Normal, Sport, Electronic

Vibration reduction. Electronic adds a crop and is not offered in R3D. Use Off on a tripod, Normal handheld, Sport for panning and vehicle work.

Res/FPS Video

Options
Every frame size and rate the camera offers in the current codec, labelled by size bucket and rate: 8K 30p, 4K 60p, 1920x1080 120p ×4

The label carries a [FX] or [DX] prefix when one size exists in two crops (ZR RAW formats), exact pixel sizes when sizes would otherwise collide (Z8 and Z9 RAW), and a ×4 or ×5 factor on slow-motion entries that the camera conforms in the file. Changing the format re-reads the ISO list.

Codec Video

Options
H.264 8b, H.265 8b, H.265 10b, N-RAW, ProRes, ProRes RAW, R3D, as the body supports

The recording format. Changing it changes the available frame sizes, the ISO list, the white balance options and the exposure tool calibration (R3D switches the tools to Log3G10). See Codecs, RAW and bit depth.

Quality Photo

Options
Basic, Basic★, Normal, Normal★, Fine, Fine★, RAW, RAW+B, RAW+N, RAW+F and their ★ variants

JPEG quality and whether a RAW file is recorded alongside. The ★ variants are the optimal-quality JPEGs. Playback groups RAW+JPEG pairs as one item.

RAW Photo

Options
Lossless, HE★, HE

RAW compression: lossless, or Nikon's high-efficiency compression in two levels.

Size Photo

Image size in pixels as the camera reports it, for example 8256x5504.

Area Photo

Options
FX, DX, 1:1, 16:9

The sensor crop. DX gives a 1.5x crop for reach; 16:9 and 1:1 for delivery shapes.

Picture Photo

Options
Standard, Neutral, Vivid, Mono, Portrait, Landscape, Flat, Auto, Flat Mono, Deep Mono, Rich Portrait, the twenty Creative Picture Controls, custom slots C-1 to C-9, and Recipe slots

The in-camera look for JPEGs. Custom slots show the name stored on the camera, for example C-1 MYLOOK New in 2.1. Video tone modes (N-Log, HLG) are not controllable from the app, because the camera does not expose them over the control link; the app infers N-Log from the codec.

Release Photo

Options
Single, CL, CH, CH+, Timer, and high-speed frame capture C15, C30, C60, C120, as the body offers

What one press of the shutter does. Timer is the camera's own self-timer; the app's countdown is on the record card. Continuous modes fire while the record button is held.

Audio meters in Live View

The AUD pill shows left and right meters with a scale from 0 down to −48, a peak hold, and colour bands: green below 72%, orange to 92%, red above. The ▲ and ▼ buttons change the camera's sensitivity in the steps above. Meters are also shown for RED cameras and HDMI sources, and per camera in Multicam. The app does not play camera audio to headphones; the meters are for level only.

Slow motion

There is no separate slow-motion tile; high-frame-rate formats appear in the Res/FPS list with a ×4 or ×5 suffix. For one-tap switching, save a format and codec under Advanced › Camera › Slow Motion Target and use the SLOW pill in Live View: on remembers the current format and writes the target; off restores the format. See Advanced settings.

Startup defaults and remembered writes

Values you set on this page are remembered and replayed when the camera reconnects, and Advanced › Camera › Startup Defaults can force a format, codec and AF behaviour before Live View starts. See Advanced settings.

Hardware triggers

While the Controls page or Live View is in front and no picker is open, these fire the record button:

  • The iPhone's volume buttons.
  • The Camera Control button on iPhones that have one (iOS 17.2 or later).
  • Bluetooth shutter remotes that act as volume keys.

Repeated presses within three quarters of a second are ignored so a remote that chatters does not start and stop a clip. The app parks the phone's volume at a mid level while the trigger is armed so both buttons produce an event, and restores it afterwards.

RED cameras

With a RED connected, the page shows RED tiles whose options come from the camera: Aperture, Angle or Shutter, ISO, ND, Kelvin, Tint, Sensor FPS, Project FPS, Format, Codec, R3D Quality and Live Stream. The record card shows TC and media remaining. See RED cameras.
