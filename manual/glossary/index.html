      Chapter 19

      Glossary.

      Every term the app and this manual use, in one or two sentences each. Terms shown in capitals are on-screen tool names.

      A to F

      AF area mode
      Which part of the frame the camera's autofocus looks at: a single point, a wide zone, the whole frame with subject detection, or a tracked subject.
      AF mode
      How the autofocus behaves: AF-S focuses once, AF-C keeps refocusing, AF-F (full-time) refocuses continuously in video, MF leaves focus to the lens ring.
      Analyze Pre-LUT
      The app's rule that scopes, zebras and false color read the camera signal before the monitoring LUT is applied, so a look cannot hide clipping.
      Anamorphic
      A lens that squeezes a wide view onto the sensor horizontally. The recorded image must be stretched back out (desqueezed) by the squeeze factor, commonly 1.33x, 1.5x, 1.8x or 2x.
      AP mode (camera Wi-Fi)
      The camera creates its own Wi-Fi network and the phone joins it. Works anywhere but has the least bandwidth and takes the phone off the internet.
      Aperture
      The adjustable opening in the lens, written as an f-number. Lower numbers let in more light and blur the background more.
      Attenuator
      A fixed pad on the audio input that lowers a loud source before it can clip the camera's preamp.
      Base ISO (native ISO)
      The ISO at which a sensor delivers its cleanest image and widest dynamic range. See dual base ISO.
      Bit depth
      How many brightness steps a file stores per channel: 8-bit is 256, 10-bit is 1024, 12-bit is 4096. Log footage needs at least 10-bit to grade without banding.
      Bonjour
      Apple's name for automatic discovery of devices on a local network. The app uses it to find Nikon cameras on Wi-Fi and to find Remote Viewer hosts.
      Bulb / Time
      Long-exposure modes for stills. Bulb exposes while the release is held; Time opens on one press and closes on the next.
      CAST (Remote Viewer)
      Share the Live View picture with other phones and tablets on the same network as read-only monitors.
      Clipping
      When a signal exceeds the maximum the file can store. Clipped highlights are solid white with no detail; clipped audio distorts.
      Code value
      The integer stored for a pixel's brightness, 0 to 255 in 8-bit or 0 to 1023 in 10-bit. Scopes convert these to IRE.
      Codec
      The compression format of a video file, such as H.264, H.265 (HEVC), ProRes, N-RAW, ProRes RAW or R3D.
      COMP (composition guides)
      Overlay grids such as rule of thirds, golden ratio, golden triangles, diagonals, centre cross and safe areas.
      Conform (playback speed)
      Playing a clip at a different frame rate than it was recorded, for example a 120 fps clip at 24 fps to see the slow motion.
      .cube
      The common text file format for 3D LUTs. The app imports .cube files into its LUT library.
      dBFS
      Decibels relative to full scale, the unit of digital audio level. 0 dBFS is the loudest possible value; dialogue usually peaks around −12 to −6 dBFS.
      Deep Scan
      A read-only capability scan of the connected camera used for support. It changes no settings.
      DSQ (desqueeze)
      Stretching the monitor image horizontally by the anamorphic squeeze factor so it shows the real frame.
      Depth of field
      The range of distances that appear sharp. It shrinks with wider apertures, longer lenses and closer subjects.
      Drop frame
      A timecode counting scheme for 29.97 and 59.94 fps that skips numbers so timecode stays in step with real time. Not used at 24, 25 or 30 fps.
      Dual base ISO
      A sensor with two native ISOs, a low base for bright scenes and a high base for dark ones. The Nikon ZR exposes the choice in R3D.
      Dynamic Island / Live Activity
      iPhone's Lock Screen and top-of-screen status area. The app shows the recording timer, long-exposure and timelapse progress there.
      EV / exposure compensation
      A stop of exposure. Exposure compensation shifts an automatic exposure by a chosen number of stops in thirds.
      EXPO (Exposure Assist)
      The tool pill that shows X-Lights and X-Posts.
      FALSE (false color)
      Replaces the picture with a colour per exposure band so every region's level is visible at once. The app offers five palettes.
      f-number / f-stop
      The aperture setting. Each full stop (1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22) halves the light.
      Focus Punch
      The camera's own magnification of the Live View image for checking focus, using the camera's zoom steps.
      Focus pull
      Moving focus from one subject to another during a shot. PULL does it between saved AF points; MF does it between saved lens positions.
      Focus stacking
      Shooting several frames at stepped focus distances to blend later for extended depth of field.
      FRME (frame guides)
      Outlines of a delivery aspect ratio over the picture, with an optional matte outside them.
      Frame rate (fps)
      Frames recorded per second. 24 for cinema, 25 and 30 for television, 50 to 120 for slow motion.
      Free Run
      Timecode that advances continuously whether or not the camera is recording, usually set to time of day.

      G to P

      Gen-1 body
      The first-generation Nikon Z cameras (Z5, Z6, Z7, Z50), which use an older control protocol. The app connects to them with a reduced control set.
      Group record
      Starting and stopping every camera in Multicam together, with learned per-camera start latency so they begin as close to the same instant as possible.
      HDMI capture card (UVC)
      A USB device that turns an HDMI output into a webcam-style video stream. On USB-C iPads and Macs the app can use one as the picture source while the camera stays connected over Wi-Fi.
      Headroom
      The gap between the loudest expected audio peak and 0 dBFS, left so surprises do not clip.
      HEVC / H.265
      A video codec with roughly half the file size of H.264 at the same quality. Available in 8-bit and 10-bit on Nikon bodies.
      HLG
      Hybrid Log-Gamma, an HDR profile that looks more finished than log but still keeps extended highlight range.
      Histogram
      A graph of how many pixels sit at each brightness. Spikes at the edges mean clipping or crushing.
      Hot corners
      Four corner buttons in the Modern Live View interface, each with a primary (tap) and secondary (long-press) tool you can assign.
      HUD
      The on-screen chrome of Live View: top bar, bottom toolbar, chips and corner buttons. Double-tap the picture to hide or show it.
      IPP2
      RED's image processing pipeline. The bundled IPP2 LUTs convert Log3G10 to Rec.709 with selectable contrast and highlight roll-off.
      IRE
      A 0 to 100 scale of video brightness used on scopes. 100 is the maximum a file can hold.
      ISO
      Sensor sensitivity (gain). Higher values brighten the image and add noise.
      Jam (timecode)
      Setting one device's timecode from a reference so that all devices agree.
      Kelvin
      The unit of colour temperature used for white balance. 3200 K tungsten, 5600 K daylight, 7000 K and above shade.
      LENS (non-CPU lens profiles)
      Saved focal length and aperture for manual lenses without electronics, written to the camera's lens-data slots so stabilisation and metadata work.
      LVL (level)
      The camera's built-in level sensor shown as a horizon line, bars or a bubble.
      Log
      A flat, low-contrast recording curve that keeps the sensor's full brightness range for grading. Nikon's is N-Log; RED's is Log3G10.
      LOOK
      The LUT browser in Live View, with bundled folders and a User folder for imported files.
      LUT
      Look-up table: a file that maps input colours to output colours. Used on the monitor to view log footage with a normal or stylised look.
      MATCH
      Exposure, gamma and contrast adjustments applied around the LUT so a look designed for one exposure suits another.
      Matte
      The darkened area outside a frame guide.
      Metering mode
      Which part of the frame the camera's light meter weighs: matrix (whole frame), centre-weighted, spot, or highlight-weighted.
      MF (Manual Focus Drive)
      Driving the lens's focus motor from the app in small steps, saving In and Out positions, and pulling between them over a chosen time.
      Middle grey
      18% reflectance, the reference exposure level. Its position on the scope depends on the profile: about 35 IRE in N-Log, 33 in Log3G10, 40 to 45 in Rec.709.
      Monitor Punch
      Magnifying the picture on the phone only (1x, 2x, 4x with pan) without telling the camera.
      MTP/PTP
      The USB mode a Nikon must be in for the app to control it over a cable. Set under the camera's Network menu.
      ND filter
      Neutral density: a grey filter that reduces light so you can keep a wide aperture and a 180° shutter in bright light. RED bodies have it built in.
      N-Log
      Nikon's log curve. Middle grey is near 35 IRE and black near 12 IRE.
      N-RAW
      Nikon's compressed RAW video format. Listed in Playback but not playable on the phone.
      RGB parade
      Three waveforms side by side for red, green and blue. The fastest way to see and match white balance.
      PEAK (focus peaking)
      Coloured outlines on the sharpest edges of the picture.
      Picture Control
      Nikon's in-camera look presets for stills (Standard, Neutral, Flat, Monochrome and so on, plus custom slots).
      Power Zoom
      Motorised zoom on PZ lenses, driven from the app with a hold-to-zoom control and adjustable speed.
      Preset
      A saved Live View layout: which tools are on, their options, toolbars, hot corners, scale and positions.
      ProRes
      Apple's editing codec. ProRes 422 HQ is lightly compressed; ProRes RAW stores sensor data.
      PTP/IP
      The network protocol Nikon cameras use for remote control over Wi-Fi.
      PULL (Focus Pull)
      Save autofocus points in the frame and move the camera's focus between them with a chosen hold time and speed.

      Q to Z

      Quick LUTs (A to D)
      Four slots on the LUT pill's long-press menu for switching looks with one tap.
      R3D
      RED's RAW format, also recorded by the Nikon ZR as R3D NE. Uses the Log3G10 curve. Not playable on the phone.
      RAW
      Sensor data stored before white balance and most processing, so those can be changed in post. Larger files; needs a compatible editor.
      RCP2
      RED's camera control protocol, used by the app over Wi-Fi or a USB-C network tether.
      Rec.709
      The standard video colour space and gamma for normal (SDR) screens. Log footage is converted to it with a LUT.
      Rec Run
      Timecode that only advances while recording.
      Rec TC
      The camera setting that records timecode into the file (On / Off / via HDMI).
      REF (reference frame)
      A captured still of the picture shown as a ghost over the live image, for matching framing between shots or days.
      Release mode
      What one press of the shutter does for stills: single frame, continuous low or high, self-timer, or high-speed frame capture.
      Remote Mode (Zf, Zfc)
      A camera setting on dial-based bodies that lets the app's exposure values override the physical dials.
      Safe area
      Inset guides (action safe, title safe) that keep important content away from edges that some screens crop.
      SCOPE
      The tool pill that shows the histogram, waveform, RGB parade or vectorscope.
      SeeMo
      Accsoon's HDMI-to-iPhone adapter. The app can use its 1080p picture as the monitor while controlling the Nikon over Wi-Fi.
      Shutter angle
      Exposure time expressed as a fraction of the frame interval. 180° is half the interval and the normal cinema setting.
      Skin line
      The diagonal on a vectorscope where all human skin tones fall. Faces off the line mean a white balance or tint error.
      SLOW
      One-tap switch to a saved slow-motion frame size and rate, and back.
      Subject detection
      The camera's recognition of people, animals, vehicles, birds or aircraft for autofocus.
      Timecode (TC)
      A frame-accurate clock recorded with the video as HH:MM:SS:FF.
      Tether session
      In Playback, automatically showing each new photo as it is taken.
      TIME
      The timelapse and intervalometer tool in photo mode, including on-device video assembly.
      Tint
      The green–magenta axis of white balance, used to correct fluorescent and LED light.
      Touch AF
      Tapping the picture to set the focus point. Instant drives focus immediately; Cinematic only moves the point.
      Traffic lights
      Another name for X-Lights.
      Vectorscope
      A scope of hue and saturation only, with colour-bar targets and a skin line.
      VR
      Nikon's vibration reduction (image stabilisation): Off, Normal, Sport, or electronic.
      Waveform
      A scope of brightness against horizontal position, with guide lines for clip, highlight, middle grey and shadow.
      White balance (WB)
      The colour the camera treats as neutral, set as a preset or in Kelvin plus tint.
      WIPE
      A split-screen comparison of the LUT and the ungraded picture with a draggable divider.
      X-Lights
      Per-channel lamps that show highlight and shadow pressure in the pre-LUT signal.
      X-Posts
      Two meters, CLIP and NOISE, summarising how close the image is to each limit.
      ZEBRA (zebras)
      Diagonal stripes painted on areas at a chosen exposure level: highlights, shadows, middle grey or all three.
      ZOOM (Punch / Zoom)
      The tool pill for Monitor Punch, Focus Punch, Power Zoom and Hi-Res Zoom.
      32-bit float
      An audio recording format with enough range that clipping is practically impossible and levels can be set in post.
      180-degree rule
      Keep the shutter angle at 180° (shutter speed of 1 over twice the frame rate) for natural motion blur.
