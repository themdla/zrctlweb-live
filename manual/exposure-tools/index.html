      Chapter 07

      Exposure tools.

      Five tools read the exposure of the picture: SCOPE, FALSE, ZEBRA, EXPO and, in photo mode, EXP. They share one idea: they measure the signal the camera records, before the LUT, and they know which log curve the camera is using. This chapter explains each tool, every option, and which one to reach for.

      How the tools are calibrated

      The thresholds for "clipped", "highlight warning", "middle grey" and "crushed shadow" are not fixed numbers. They depend on the recording profile:

      • R3D / Log3G10 on the Nikon ZR and RED bodies. The highlight thresholds follow the ISO and the active base ISO.
      • N-Log on Nikon bodies, using Nikon's published curve: black near 12%, middle grey near 35 IRE, clip at the top of the range. N-Log is assumed whenever the codec reports it or the selected LUT's name contains "N-Log".
      • SDR (Rec.709 and similar) when neither applies.

      The calibration is refreshed when you change the codec, the ISO, the base ISO or the LUT, and frozen while recording so a mid-take change cannot move the guides. With Analyze Pre-LUT on (the default, in Advanced › Monitoring › Scopes), every tool reads the log signal under the LUT; turn it off and they read the LUT output instead.

      Why this matters

      A contrasty LUT makes an overexposed sky look fine. Because the tools read the recorded signal, zebras and false color still show the clip. Trust the tools, not the look.

      SCOPE: histogram, waveform, parade, vectorscope

      Tap
      Scope on or off, using the last type (Waveform by default)
      Long-press
      Histogram, Waveform, RGB Parade, Vectorscope; tap the active one to turn the scope off. Opacity: 100%, 75%, 50%, 35% (default 100%)
      Size
      Drag the ↔ handle or pinch the panel, 0.85x to 2.4x (default 1.35x). Double-tap the panel to reset. Also Scope Size in Advanced
      Mode
      Video and photo

      The scope floats over the picture and can be long-pressed and dragged anywhere. The waveform and parade draw dashed guide lines with their 8-bit code values on the right: C (red) is the highlight clip point, H (yellow) the highlight warning, M (green) middle grey, S (red) the shadow clip point, with an orange shadow-warning line. In N-Log pre-LUT mode S is pinned to code 32 and C to 255; in other profiles the lines move with ISO and base ISO.

      Histogram

      Red, green, blue and luma counts of how many pixels sit at each brightness, with the calibration bands shaded. A pile-up at the right edge is clipping; at the left edge, crushed shadows.

      When to use it

      A quick overall check, especially for stills. It does not tell you where in the frame the problem is; use the waveform or false color for that.

      Waveform

      Brightness plotted against horizontal position, so the scope lines up with the picture left to right. Read the level of a face or a wall by finding its column.

      When to use it

      The default for video exposure. Put faces around the M line plus one stop (the gap between M and H is roughly two stops in log), keep the brightest thing you care about below C, and keep important shadows above S.

      RGB Parade

      Three waveforms side by side for red, green and blue. For a neutral object the three traces should sit at the same height.

      When to use it

      White balance and camera matching. If blue sits higher than red on a grey wall, the image is too cool: raise the Kelvin. If green sits apart from the other two, adjust tint. In Multicam, put a grey card in front of each camera and match the parades.

      Vectorscope

      Hue and saturation only, on a BT.709 graticule with 75% targets for red, yellow, green, cyan, blue and magenta, a skin-tone line, and a gain note (the display is amplified so small saturations are visible).

      When to use it

      Skin tones and colour balance. A face should fall on the skin line whatever the ethnicity; if it sits to one side, white balance or tint is off. A grey card should sit dead centre. Log footage is low in saturation, so the trace looks small; that is normal.

      FALSE: false color

      Tap
      False color on or off, using the style chosen in settings
      Long-press
      Off, ZR Calibrated, IRE Spectrum, Cine Classic, Clip Guard, X-Zone
      Default style
      X-Zone
      Legend
      Shown by default (False Color Legend in Advanced); drag it anywhere
      Exclusive with
      Focus peaking and zebras. Turning false color on turns peaking off, and the reverse

      False color replaces the picture with a colour per exposure band. Each style has its own set of bands; the legend lists them top to bottom from brightest to darkest.

      StyleBands (top to bottom)Best for
      X-ZoneThirteen one-stop bands from +6 through 0 (middle grey) to −6, scaled to the active log curveLighting and ratio work: you can read how many stops a face is above a wall at a glance. The default.
      ZR CalibratedClip, Warn Hi, Near Hi, Upper Mid, Mid Gray, Low Mid, Shadow, Deep ShadowEveryday exposure on the ZR with the thresholds tuned to its log.
      IRE SpectrumClip, Warn Hi, Near Hi, Upper Hi, Skin Hi, Mid Gray, Low Mid, Shadow, Deep ShadowThe familiar rainbow palette with a dedicated skin band, if you come from other monitors.
      Cine ClassicClip, Near Hi, Skin +1, Mid Gray, Shadow, Deep ShadowA six-band palette that stays calm: most of the frame keeps its colour and only the landmarks are painted.
      Clip GuardOver, Mid Gray, UnderThe simplest check: three colours for clipped, correct and crushed. Good for fast run-and-gun.
      When to use it

      Setting up a shot or lighting a scene, when you want to see the exposure of everything at once. It is less useful while rolling because the picture itself disappears; switch to zebras or X-Lights once exposure is set. The style can also be changed in Advanced › Monitoring › Scopes.

      ZEBRA: zebras

      Tap
      Zebras on or off
      Long-press
      Highlights, Shadows, Mid Gray, All (default All). Picking a mode does not itself turn zebras on
      Exclusive with
      False color

      Stripes are painted where the picture reaches a calibrated level. The colours are fixed: red for clipped highlights, yellow for the highlight warning, magenta for crushed shadows, blue for the shadow warning, green for the middle-grey band. There are no threshold sliders; the levels come from the profile, ISO and LUT calibration, which is the same calibration the scope guides use.

      When to use it

      While rolling. Zebras leave the picture intact and only paint the problem areas. Use Highlights to protect skies and windows, Mid Gray to put a grey card or a face at the reference level, Shadows to catch important darks falling into noise, and All for everything at once.

      EXPO: X-Lights and X-Posts

      Tap
      On or off, using the last mode (X-Lights + X-Posts by default)
      Long-press
      X-Lights + X-Posts, X-Lights Only, X-Posts Only; tap the active one to turn off
      Needs
      A connected camera (Nikon, RED or an HDMI source)

      X-Lights are three columns, one each for red, green and blue. Each column has an upper lamp for highlight pressure, a segmented bar, and a lower lamp for shadow pressure. A lamp lights as that channel approaches its limit in the pre-LUT signal. Because they are per channel, a saturated red light that clips only the red channel is caught before the luma waveform would show it.

      X-Posts are two sixteen-segment meters: CLIP (red) for how much of the frame is at or near the clip point and NOISE (orange) for how much is down in the noise floor.

      The panel floats over the picture and can be dragged.

      When to use it

      The "traffic lights" for shooting: glance at the lamps while watching the picture. Keep all lamps off or amber; a red upper lamp means close the aperture or add ND; a red lower lamp means open up or add light. Pair it with zebras to find where the problem is.

      EXP: the camera's exposure meter Photo

      Tap
      Meter overlay on or off (on by default)
      Range
      −3 to +3 EV, with arrows beyond

      A draggable METER panel showing the camera's own light meter: a green marker when balanced within half a stop, orange when over, blue when under, with the value in EV. In manual mode it shows the meter reading; in P, S and A it shows the exposure compensation.

      When to use it

      Stills in manual mode, when you want the camera's opinion of the scene without looking at the camera. For video, use the scopes instead; the meter does not know about log.

      Which tool when

      SituationTool
      Lighting a scene, setting ratiosFalse color (X-Zone) to read stops across the frame.
      Setting exposure for a takeWaveform with the C/H/M/S guides; place faces one stop above M.
      Protecting a bright sky or windowZebras on Highlights, or Clip Guard false color.
      RollingZebras plus X-Lights, so the picture stays visible.
      White balance and matching camerasRGB parade on a grey card; vectorscope for skin.
      Checking a LUT is not hiding anythingAny tool with Analyze Pre-LUT on (the default), or WIPE to see the raw log beside the look.
      Stills, manual modeHistogram plus the EXP meter.
